About

The person behind the books

The rails, the trade, the writing

Matthew Hogan was sixteen when he left home and learned to hop trains. For eight years he hitchhiked and rode freight across America—46 states, five Canadian provinces, one accidental border crossing into Canada that taught him more about momentum than geography ever did. The rails were his first education: how to read a schedule, how to read a stranger, how to trust the thin edge between risk and reward.

His mother, Michelle Kennedy, wrote Without A Net—a memoir about raising him and his siblings while living out of a van. Storytelling wasn't a career choice in his family. It was how you made sense of things.

When the traveling ended, he enrolled at Eastern Maine Community College and spent eight years in the electrical trade—wiring, troubleshooting, building systems that had to work because people's lights depended on them. The same instinct now runs through his code: simple tools, no waste, nothing between the maker and the thing made.

Today he writes from the Witch's Foot—a thin stretch of Maine coast where the Atlantic chews the shore and the sky doesn't bother with subtlety. He builds websites by hand on $0 compute. He publishes his books through KDP. He answers his own emails. The rails taught him how to travel light, and he never stopped.

His work includes The Ealdforn Sagas, Cowboys of Chivalry, The Lost Tales of Telume, and the Digital Divination series. Fantasy, western, mythos—different worlds, same voice.
